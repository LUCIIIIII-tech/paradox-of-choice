# Paradox of Choice — treatment

Sci-Fi Sitcom Toon, 1080×1920 (9:16), 24 fps, about 45 s. English dialogue (two voices, Kokoro offline), burned-in captions plus `.srt`. Inspired by adult-swim-era sci-fi sitcom cartoons (grammar only).

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A. The falling stack (chosen).** Nervous roommate can't choose between two sandwiches. Deadpan inventor roommate "solves" it with a portal: a deli world with 30 sandwiches, then an endless aisle of 3,000, then the floor gives way and he falls down a vertical stack of worlds, each with more options, until he lands in a gas station with one sad sandwich. He is happy at last. She names the effect. Ending: he refuses to go home.
- Uses the vertical frame as the medium: worlds are floors stacked on top of each other, and the fall is the machine-gun.

**B. The fridge that grows.** One world, many doors: every press of the remote adds shelves to the fridge, which grows taller than the apartment. The kitchen becomes a cathedral of sandwiches. Strong single location, but no palette jumps, so it loses the style's main read ("we jumped" from colour alone).

**C. Tech-support call.** Benny is stuck in an infinite deli, Oona guides him by phone from home. Two worlds on screen at once forces a split screen, which this vertical format and this channel avoid. Rejected.

**Why A.** The topic is literally "more options, less happiness", so the number of options is the escalation and the fall is the native move: one continuous downward oner where only the palette and the count change. The ending is the anti-climax the psychology predicts: the single option wins.

## 2. Logline and arc

A roommate paralysed by two sandwiches is sent through ever bigger worlds of choice, and only finds peace in a gas station that sells exactly one.

- **Setup**: Benny at the open fridge: ham or turkey. Forty minutes. Oona, deadpan, presses a button on a remote duct-taped to a colander.
- **Turn**: more options make it worse each time (30, 3,000, then a fall through a million, a billion, infinity). He lands in beige silence with one sandwich.
- **Ending (stuck elsewhere)**: "It's perfect." It is week-old egg salad. "I didn't have to choose." Oona names the paradox of choice. "Coming home?" He shakes his head: "Home has two sandwiches." She shrugs and leaves through the portal. He stays, happily, in the beige world with one option. Title.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Adult-swim-era sci-fi sitcom cartoons.** Learn: dialogue-driven comedy, reaction close-ups after every absurd image, dead air as a punchline, tech as household junk. Don't take: any character, silhouette, colour combo, catchphrase, portal look or logo.
- **Don Hertzfeldt, *World of Tomorrow*.** Learn: deadpan delivery against cosmic scale, the long held pause that lets an existential line land, a tiny human want inside an enormous universe. Don't take: the stick-figure design, the voices, any line.

## 4. Cast and devices

- **Benny** (nervous lead): short, pear-shaped, tiny curl of hair, wears a coral travel neck-pillow at all times (comfort item) over a mustard cardigan. No glasses. Higher, faster voice (`am_puck`, presence boost). Panic jitter grows with the number of options.
- **Oona** (deadpan lead): very tall, slouched, heavy lids, a tall plum beehive bun with a pencil stuck through it, wears oven mitts all the time ("the tech runs hot"). Forest-green dungarees over a striped shirt. Low, flat, slow voice (a Kokoro female voice cast by measured f0, slowed, pitched down, gravel).
- **The Elsewhere Remote**: a TV remote duct-taped to a colander, one big button. Its portal is the only glow in the film: **cyan-white**, used nowhere else. Shape: a rounded rectangle (it first opens inside the fridge door frame); in the fall, flat elliptical holes in each floor.
- **The Special** (creature, world 2): a sentient hoagie with a lettuce tongue and one googly eye. Non-verbal: glorp, slurp.

## 8. Cue map

**Score shape: more options, more instruments.** One theme (D minor, theremin) that piles up layers with every world, until the fall plays everything at once and too fast; then silence; then the theme returns on one instrument. The music is the paradox.

Grid at 120 bpm (one beat = 0.5 s, bar = 2 s) for home, the deli worlds and the end; deli-verse 2 at 144, the fall accelerates to 168. Every world cut lands on a downbeat.

| Section | Time | Tempo | Instruments | Hits on the beat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home | 0–9 | 120 | 2 layers: theremin lead + analog bass, drum machine kick | 0.0 impact; portal open on a downbeat |
| Deli-verse 1 | ~11–16 | 120 | 4 layers: + brushed swing kit, vibes | landing; "Pick one" on beat 1 |
| Deli-verse 2 | ~16–20 | 144 | 6 layers: + surf guitar (Karplus–Strong), square arpeggio | glorp |
| Fall | ~20–24 | 168 | everything at once + kazoo stinger per floor, shorter each time, tremolo strings | each floor portal |
| Gas station | ~24–31 | — | **silence**, only fluorescent hum and heat-lamp buzz | first sound after silence: plastic-wrap crinkle + "It's perfect" |
| Bliss / explain | ~31–41 | 90 | 1 layer: the theme on a lone warm Rhodes | tag types on the beat |
| Title | ~43–47 | 120 | 1 layer: the theme's last phrase on theremin alone, one kick on the hit | title pops on the downbeat |

## 10. Subtitles and title

- Captions: Baloo 2 ExtraBold ~58 px, white with an ~11 px purple-black outline, centred at y ≈ 1500–1640 (above the phone UI, below faces). A speaker pill on the left in the character's colour: Benny mustard, Oona green. Lines over ~900 px wrap to two lines. Clipped at world changes and before each silence.
- World tags: VT323 terminal type, typed in, top-left, coloured per world: `HOME · 2 OPTIONS`, `DELI-VERSE 1 · 30 OPTIONS`, `DELI-VERSE 2 · 3,000 OPTIONS`, `GAS STATION · 1 OPTION`. During the explanation: `PARADOX OF CHOICE (SCHWARTZ, 2004)`.
- Title card: "PARADOX OF CHOICE" in chunky period display lettering (Lilita One), cream letters on plum, coral drop shadow, thick boiling outline, popped letter by letter on 12 fps steps on the downbeat. No acid green, no slime, no bubble lettering.
- No credit or watermark text in frame.
